Portrait — Knight
27 years · 6 designing · 15 playing

From loss landscapes

I was born in Bengaluru in 1998. I grew up drawing on the backs of notebooks and learning chess from a grandfather who never let me win — two habits that taught me the same thing: look at the whole board before you move.

At university I studied Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning. I learned to see systems — gradients, weights, the strange beauty of a model converging. But somewhere in my third year I realised I was more interested in the person on the other side of the model than the model itself. The interface was the problem I actually wanted to solve.

So I pivoted. I moved into product design — first at a research startup, now as a product designer at a mid-stage scale-up. My ML training didn't go to waste: it's why I design with an intuition for what the system underneath is actually doing. Trained in how machines think. Working on how people feel.

2. Nf3 — Development

Four sides of the same player.

Every piece on the board is me. The queen does the visible work, but the knight, bishop and king are always in play.

i. The day job

Product Designer

Versatile, far-reaching, the piece that does the most work. I build products that earn their place — from research to shipped interface, without losing the soul of the idea. This is my queen: what I do now, and what I want to be known for.

ii. The foundation

AI / ML Graduate

Slower, heavier, essential. My degree in machine learning taught me how systems think — gradients, loss landscapes, the strange beauty of a model converging. It's the king behind the design: quieter than the work, but everything rests on it.

iii. The mind

Chess Player

Doesn't move in straight lines. Sees patterns, anticipates three moves out, and trusts the position over the plan. Chess is how I learned to think about strategy — and strategy is a design material too.

iv. The eye

Artist & Sketcher

Travels on diagonals. Before Figma there was a sketchbook, and before the sketchbook there was a pencil. Drawing is how I think out loud — the fastest way to get a half-formed idea out of my head and into a form I can argue with.
